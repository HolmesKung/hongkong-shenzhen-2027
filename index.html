Kwan Tai Temple

深水埗關帝廟

A neighbourhood temple from the late Qing era

The Chinese Temples Committee dates the temple to around 1891. It honours Guan Yu, a general associated with the Three Kingdoms era. Remembered for loyalty and martial courage, he came to be worshipped as Kwan Tai, also known as Mo Tai. Both names appear in references to this temple.

On Hoi Tan Street, the small entrance is easy to miss among the surrounding shops. The scale is part of the experience. This is a neighbourhood place of worship and a quiet stop before Disneyland.

Hong Kong Disneyland

香港迪士尼樂園

How the park grew after 2005

Hong Kong Disneyland opened on Lantau Island in September 2005. The park gradually added experiences of its own, including Mystic Manor in Mystic Point. The original castle was reimagined as the Castle of Magical Dreams in 2020, and World of Frozen opened in 2023.

Compare the castle, the imagined world of Arendelle, and the eccentric collector’s home in Mystic Manor. Each shows a different era of the park’s development.

Che Kung Temple

沙田車公廟

A commander remembered as a protector

Che Kung was a military commander associated with the Southern Song dynasty. In local tradition, Sha Tin villagers sought his protection during an epidemic. That story helps explain why the Tai Wai temple has become a place to ask for good health and fortune.

Watch for the windmills inside. Turning one is associated with a change in fortune, especially around Lunar New Year. The tradition is still part of religious practice for many visitors.

Wong Tai Sin Temple

嗇色園黃大仙祠

From a shrine to a pilgrimage site

The temple’s operator traces the Kowloon site to 1921, when worshippers established a temple beneath Lion Rock. Its tradition honours Wong Tai Sin while drawing on Taoism, Buddhism and Confucianism.

Before the altar, you may see people shake a container of numbered bamboo sticks until one falls out. This practice, called kau chim, is a way to seek guidance and is part of active worship.

Man Mo Temple

文武廟

A temple and meeting place on Hollywood Road

Chinese merchants built the compound between 1847 and 1862. Man Cheong is associated with literature and scholarship, while Mo Tai is associated with martial affairs. The neighbouring Lit Shing Kung was used for worship of other deities and Kung Sor served as a meeting place for community affairs.

Notice the low light and hanging incense coils as you enter. The compound’s three buildings show how worship and local civic life shared the same address in nineteenth-century Sheung Wan.

Repulse Bay shrine area

淺水灣天后及觀音像

Maritime beliefs on the south coast

Repulse Bay sits on the south side of Hong Kong Island. Near the beach is a traditional-style complex associated with the Hong Kong China Life Saving Society. Its shrine area includes Tin Hau, the sea goddess traditionally revered by sailors and fishing communities, and Kwun Yum, associated with compassion.

Follow the beachfront to the brightly coloured statues. Their position beside the sea gives context to the maritime beliefs represented here. The shrine is beside the beach facilities, which are managed separately.

Nantou Ancient City

南头古城

A former coastal stronghold

Nantou’s administrative history goes back to 331, during the Eastern Jin dynasty. Over the centuries the area served as a regional centre, a coastal fortress and a point of exchange for maritime trade. It is also known as Xin’an Old Town.

Begin at the south gate and look for older masonry and narrow streets as you pass cafés and contemporary shops. The district’s long history and its current everyday use are both visible on the same walk.

Zhongshuge

钟书阁（欢乐港湾店）

A bookstore designed around a spiral

The Shenzhen branch was designed by X+Living. Its long, horizontal spiral turns a bookcase into the main feature of the shop. The designers connected its sense of movement to Shenzhen’s rapid development and the passage of time.

Look down the centre of the spiral, then view it from the side. The changing perspective helps explain why the shop is a destination in its own right. It is also an operating bookstore, so keep aisles clear.

Happy Harbour

欢乐港湾

Bao’an’s public waterfront

OH Bay brings together a waterfront park, pedestrian corridors, shops and places to eat. It shows how this part of Bao’an has developed public leisure space along the coast. The wheel is its most visible landmark.

Walk from the East Bank shops onto the promenade. The open shoreline offers room for a slower walk and a break between the bookstore and the observation wheel.

Bay Glory

湾区之光摩天轮

A modern landmark over the estuary

Bay Glory began operating in 2021 and rises about 128 metres above OH Bay. Shenzhen’s government describes the view as taking in Qianhai Bay and the Pearl River estuary, with the city and waterfront visible in the same sweep.

On a clear day, look for the shoreline and the new developments across the bay. The promenade also gives a good view of the wheel from below if you skip the ride.

MOCAPE

深圳市当代艺术与城市规划馆

Contemporary art and the city’s growth

Shenzhen’s Museum of Contemporary Art and Urban Planning brings two institutions together in one Futian building. The art programme looks outward to contemporary practice, while the planning exhibits focus on how Shenzhen has been built and how its districts continue to change.

Spend a moment in the large atrium before choosing a gallery. Visiting both sides gives you two ways into the same city, through its art and through the plans that shaped it.

Shenzhen Civic Center

深圳市民中心

The public square on Futian’s central axis

Civic Center Square forms part of the public landscape running through central Futian. Its broad open space and the wing-like roof make the civic complex easy to recognise, while the surrounding institutions make it a meeting point for culture and public administration.

Step back into the plaza to take in the scale of the building. It is a useful midpoint between the art and planning museum and the history museum.

Shenzhen Museum

深圳博物馆（历史民俗馆）

Local life before and during rapid urban change

The History and Folk Culture branch is in Civic Center’s Zone A. It helps connect present-day Shenzhen to the communities that lived in the region before its recent growth. Displays of local customs and everyday objects sit alongside material about the city’s modern development.

For a shorter visit, pick one gallery on folk life and one on the city’s development. Looking at both makes it easier to connect the modern skyline with what came before.

Use this stop to see a different side of the district after the museums. The restaurant and café areas make a convenient place to pause and regroup.

Hong Kong emergency numbers · Mainland emergency numbers

Before departure

  • Crossing the border

    Hong Kong and mainland China have separate immigration procedures. Carry your own passport. Check both jurisdictions’ current entry requirements for each traveler, including Hong Kong re-entry on 24 Jan.

  • Payments & internet

    Keep some cash as backup. Mainland internet access differs from Hong Kong; confirm roaming and app access in advance.

During the trip

  • Stay together

    Agree on a meeting point before entering stations or attractions. Use a group chat and buddy pairs. Tell the group before leaving a venue.

  • Weather & comfort

    January is generally cool, around 12–21°C across the two cities, with occasional colder spells. Dress in layers and check forecasts shortly before departure.

  • Temple etiquette

    Dress respectfully, speak quietly and follow photography and incense rules. Donations are optional. Be considerate of worshippers.

Bookings & station names

  • Confirm hotel booking details, train tickets, museum reservation requirements, Disney operating calendar and the final airport transfer. The tour leader should share the final meeting times and contact number with everyone.
  • Station names for Amap. Use 福田站 for Futian High-Speed Railway Station. 福田口岸 is the Futian Checkpoint. West Kowloon and Kowloon Airport Express are connected on foot. Allow 10–20 minutes between them with luggage.
